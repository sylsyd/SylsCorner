How to play

The one idea

A coordinating conjunction (the FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) joins two clauses that could each stand alone as a sentence. Both stay on the ground.

A subordinating conjunction (because, although, while, if, when, since, unless, until, before, after…) takes a clause and makes it dependent. It lifts off the ground and has to hang off a main clause. Left alone, it falls: that's a fragment.

Three rounds

  • Sort: words drop, send each one left (coordinating) or right (subordinating). Speed climbs.
  • Build: join real clauses. Structure points for the right type, sense bonus for the word that actually fits the meaning.
  • Judge: is the sentence complete, a fragment, or two sentences crashed together?

For the teacher

  • Brainrot: off swaps every slang line for plain English. Same game, observation-safe.
  • Whole class on the board: round 1 with hands up for left / right, then pairs for rounds 2 and 3.
  • The end card gives the aura breakdown plus three discussion questions.
  • Watch for for, so, since and as. This game treats FANBOYS for and so as coordinating and since/as as subordinating, which is the KS3 line, but they shift with use and that's worth saying out loud.

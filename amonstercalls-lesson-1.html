A Monster Calls

Today

  1. Meet the book

You need: the novel, your exercise book, a pen.

Students write the date and "A Monster Calls: Chapters 1-2" as a title in their books while you hand out copies. Arrow keys or Space to move on. Press N (or the button bottom left) to show or hide these notes.
Step 1 · The first line · 2 min

This is how the book starts:

"The monster showed up just after midnight. As they do."
Talk to your partner · 1 minute
  1. When does the monster arrive?
  2. "As they do" means monsters always come at night. Does that sound scary, funny or calm?
  3. Guess: what kind of story is this going to be?

Say it like this: "I think this story will be ... because ..."

take 2 or 3 guesses. Any answer with a reason is fine. Point out "As they do" sounds almost bored: the narrator is calm about a monster.
Step 2 · Where we are going · 3 min

In Week 7 you will write an essay on ONE of these

Theme A

"Family is a recurring theme in A Monster Calls."

Family = Conor, his mum, his grandma, his dad. How do they treat each other? Do they change?

Theme B

"Supernatural vs. Reality is a recurring theme in A Monster Calls."

Supernatural = things that cannot happen in real life, like a tree that walks. Reality = real life. Is the monster real or not?

Recurring = it comes back again and again in the book.
So every time we read, we look for clues for both themes.

do not explain more than this today. Tomorrow's lesson (topic vs theme) and the Theme tracker build on it.
Step 3 · Before we read · 2 min

Who's who and words to know

Who's who

  • Conor O'Malley: 13 years old, lives with his mum
  • Mum: ill, having treatment
  • Grandma: Mum's mother
  • Dad: lives in America

Words to know

  • yew tree: a very old tree with poisonous red berries
  • graveyard: where people are buried, next to a church
  • treatment: medicine or hospital care for a serious illness
  • fortnightly: every two weeks
  • settee: sofa · rucksack: school bag
the illness is not named yet in the book. If asked, say "we will find out more as we read". Be aware some students may have family members who are ill.
Step 4 · Read · 9 min

Chapter 1: "A Monster Calls"

Follow in your book while I read

Listen for

  1. What time is it when the monster comes?
  2. Is Conor scared of the monster?

We will stop twice

Stop 1: "Someone was calling his name."
Who could be calling?

Stop 2: end of the chapter.
Thumbs up if Conor is scared. Thumbs down if not.

read aloud. Stop 1 is about a third of the way in. Stop 2: most will see he is not scared; ask "Why not?" and leave it open. The answer comes in Question 7.
Step 5 · True or false · 3 min

Stand up if it is TRUE. Stay sitting if it is FALSE.

Click each sentence to show the answer.

1. The monster arrives at 12.07.

TRUE

2. Conor has told his mum about his nightmare.

FALSE: he has told no one

3. The monster is made from a yew tree.

TRUE

4. Conor screams and runs away.

FALSE: he says "So come and get me then"

5. Conor is thirteen years old.

TRUE: he turned 13 last month

6. The monster smashes Conor's window.

TRUE... but wait for Chapter 2!
number 6 is the hook. It happens in Chapter 1, but in the morning the window is not broken. Do not explain; tell them to watch for it.
Step 6 · Read · 10 min

Chapter 2: "Breakfast"

Follow in your book while I read

Listen for

  1. What does Conor find on his bedroom floor?
  2. What jobs does Conor do by himself?

We will stop twice

Stop 1: "Every inch of his bedroom floor was covered in short, spiky yew tree leaves."
Real or dream? Thumbs up = real. Thumbs down = dream.

Stop 2: end of the chapter.
How does Mum seem?

at Stop 1, link back to True or false number 6: the window is fine, but the leaves are there. At Stop 2, accept "tired", "ill", "sad", "worried".

  1. Write the question number.

Step 9 · Reading between the lines · 4 min

The writer doesn't always tell us. We find a clue and work it out.

Example

"Sam slams the door and throws his bag on the floor."

→
What it tells us

Sam is angry.

Now together. What does each clue tell us? Click to check.

a) "His chewing was the only sound in the whole house." → The house is silent. Conor eats alone; Mum is still in bed.

b) "Conor would have to make his own breakfast, something he'd grown used to doing." → This happens a lot. Conor often looks after himself.

c) "She hadn't tied her scarf around her head yet ... her bare scalp" → Mum has lost her hair because of her treatment. She is seriously ill.

take answers before clicking. Praise any answer that points to the clue word ("grown used to" = it happens often).
Step 10 · Questions 7-8: reading between the lines · 8 min · in your book
7

Conor is not scared of the monster. What could be worse than a monster for Conor?

"Shout all you want," Conor shrugged ... "I've seen worse."
Conor says "I've seen worse." This suggests that ... because ...
Go back to the very start of Chapter 1. What had Conor just woken up from? Who has he told about it?
8

Why does Mum wish Conor didn't have to be "so good"?

"You're a good boy," she said again. "I wish you didn't have to be quite so good."
Mum wishes this because ... This shows that at home ...
Look at your answer to Question 6. In most families, who usually does those jobs? Why can't Mum do them?

Finished? Mum smiles when she calls him "a good boy", but how does she really feel? Find the word that shows it.

these two are the important ones. Circulate to the students who need the most help first and point them to the frame. Fast-finisher answer: "he could hear sadness in it".
Step 11 · Check your answers · 4 min

Click each number. Tick it, or fix it in a different colour.

  1. Clock When Conor wakes up, the clock says 12.07, seven minutes past midnight.
  2. Nightmare Conor has told no one: not his mum, his dad, his grandma or anyone at school.
  3. Monster The monster is made of the old yew tree from the graveyard on the hill.
  4. Floor In the morning, Conor finds his floor covered in short, spiky yew tree leaves.
  5. Leaves He sweeps them up, puts them in a plastic bag and hides it at the bottom of the bin.
  6. Jobs Any three: makes his own breakfast, rinses the dishes, takes out the rubbish and recycling, puts the sheets in the washing machine, gets ready for school.
  7. Worse This suggests something scares him more than the monster, like his nightmare, which he keeps secret. It may be linked to his mum being ill.
  8. So good Conor does jobs a parent usually does because Mum is ill and tired from her treatment. At home, he has had to grow up fast.
if time is short, skip this step and open Tuesday's lesson with it.

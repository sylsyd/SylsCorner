Warm-up. Tap Next letter slowly and let them shout guesses after two letters. Make them say the letters aloud in English (they get the alphabet practice for free). When the word is out, ask: "What do you notice?" You want: the first letters of the lines spell the word. That is the whole definition of an acrostic.
Language

How an acrostic works

Parts, a quick check, then level up your lines. Les parties, un test rapide, puis on améliore les lignes.

RULE 1Write a word down the page.Écris un mot à la verticale.
RULE 2One letter, one line.Une lettre, une ligne.
RULE 3A line can be a word, a phrase or a sentence.Un mot, un groupe de mots ou une phrase.
RULE 4Rhymes are fine, but you don't need them.Les rimes sont possibles, pas obligatoires.
0 / 0Does the line start with the letter?
Language. Tab 1: tap each letter in the spine so the line lights up, and ask "What is the first letter of this line?" Tab 2 is a fast whole-class check, the fixes are spoken. Tab 3 is the key skill for the writing stage: a word becomes a phrase when you add who, when or where, and a phrase becomes a sentence when you add a subject, a verb or because. Ask students to say a level 3 line for their own first letter.

Speaking. The roulette is the fun part and also the vocabulary part: every word said is a word for someone's poem. Give each team a turn per spin; no repeated words. 3Level up

Take 3 of your words. Write each one 3 times: word, phrase, sentence.

talkative → Talkative with my friends → Talkative at lunch because I love my friends.

sporty → Sporty on Saturdays → Sporty because I play badminton every Saturday.

5My partner

Write a poem for your partner's name. Use he or she. Use the interview answers.

6My name poem

Words. One word on each line: adjectives, hobbies, things you like.

  • Capital letter at the start
  • At least 2 adjectives
  • At least 1 hobby

Phrases. 3 to 6 words on each line. Add who, when or where.

  • Talkative with my friends
  • Swimming on Saturdays
  • At least 1 how often word

Sentences. A full sentence on each line.

  • 1 because sentence
  • 1 how often word
  • 1 play / go / do hobby

Extension

A. Coat of arms

Choose a value word: BRAVE, KIND, LOYAL, WISE or FREE. Write an acrostic for it.

B. Rhyme time

Change two lines of your name poem so that they rhyme.

C. Title and last line

Add a title and one extra line at the end that sums you up.

Writing. All tasks are visible at once, so students can work through them at their own pace. Tasks 1 and 2 build the raw material, task 3 is the level-up skill, task 4 is a low-stakes rehearsal, task 5 reuses the interview, and task 6 is the main poem. Choose the tier for task 6 from the Week 1 diagnostic: A is words, B is phrases, C is sentences. Extension A links to the coat of arms in the unit booklet. Tap Model answers to mark tasks 3 and 4.

Optional homework: write an acrostic for someone at home. Say why you chose each line.

Wrap-up. Do one puzzle as a class, then let pairs race on the second. For the last 3 minutes, students swap notebooks and use the Share and say frames: underline the best line and tell the writer why. Finish with the exit ticket: tap a button to cycle through Not yet, Nearly and Yes. The students who put Not yet on the last item get the A tier next lesson.
